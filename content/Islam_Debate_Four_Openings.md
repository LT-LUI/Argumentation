# Islam Is a Religion of Peace: the four opening statements

**Streamlined speeches → bullet points → standard-form arguments**  
Prepared 4 October 2026

This document uses only the four opening statements in the supplied transcript, in their original speaking order: Zeba Khan, Ayaan Hirsi Ali, Maajid Nawaz and Douglas Murray. It does not draw on the later exchanges or closing statements.

The first section is an edited paraphrase in each speaker's voice, **not a verbatim quotation**. ## 1. Streamlined versions

### Zeba Khan · For the motion

I begin with my own experience as an American Muslim. I grew up in a religious family and a mosque where men and women prayed together and a woman served as president. My parents expressed their faith through devotion and community service. They encouraged knowledge, questions and critical thinking. They understood Islam to teach freedom of religion and respect for other communities, and sent us to a Hebrew day school for nine years.

Mine is one of 1.5 billion Muslim stories. Muslims differ in denomination, schools of thought, religious observance and political outlook. Our scriptures contain passages about peace, justice and love, but also passages about violence. To decide whether Islam is a religion of peace, we must therefore examine how people practice and interpret it.

Gallup's study describes 93% of Muslims as peaceful and mainstream, and 7% as politically radicalized; only a smaller proportion of that latter group turns to violence. What distinguishes the radicalized group is politics, not piety. Robert Pape's research likewise finds political motivations for terrorism. The Tamil Tigers, for example, used suicide bombing for secessionist rather than religious objectives. The violent minority of Muslims is motivated by politics, not religion.

Our opponents would have you believe that Islam and the West are locked in conflict, and that combining Islamic and Western identities produces an intolerable contradiction. That does not describe my family, friends or community. These identities were never in conflict for us.

Violent criminals twist our faith to justify hatred, but they do not speak for Islam. Muslims who rescue others, protect Jewish strangers, support women and reject terrorists' claims speak for it. Consider Mohammad Hamdani, who died as a first responder on 9/11; Hassan Askari, who defended a Jewish stranger; Zainab Salbi's work assisting women; and the Indian Muslim community's refusal to bury the Mumbai attackers alongside other Muslims.

The media and those invested in a conflict between Islam and the West do not tell these stories. Our limited visibility does not mean that we are absent or silent. Voting against the motion would reject mainstream moderate voices and tell terrorists that you accept their version of Islam. Vote for the motion.

### Ayaan Hirsi Ali · Against the motion

This motion is not merely an academic question. I admire Zeba Khan's pluralistic upbringing and her integration into American society. But I do not accept that her example establishes that she represents Islam. Who speaks for Islam: Khan, or Faisal Shahzad, another middle-class person with an apparently integrated life who chose violence through a combination of piety and politics? The question of who speaks for Islam remains unresolved.

Religion is humanity's search for the sacred, expressed intellectually, through practice and through fellowship. Intellectually, Islam began by rejecting other gods and seeking to unite humanity under one God. No, monotheistic religion is a religion of peace, and certainly not Islam: such religions have long periods of peace but also long periods of war.

In practice, Islam includes charity, pilgrimage and prayer, but also jihad, which was central to its conquests and success. Muhammad conducted 65 successful military campaigns. This history of militarism must be considered alongside the legacy of empire. Conquest brought together people of different ethnicities and languages; even after imperial decline, that legacy leaves a high likelihood of conflict, particularly along communal fault lines. Militarism and imperial legacy alone contradict the motion.

There are two further factors: victimhood and absolutism. Defeat and imperial decline can produce a sense of victimhood, which leaders exploit by claiming that Islam is under siege. I acknowledge that those adopting this outlook are a minority. Muslims were indeed conquered, colonized and humiliated, but explanations that blame outsiders alone exclude the possibility of internal flaws.

Muslims once led in science and mathematics. Why did they not lead the later scientific revolution? Was everything caused by outsiders, or did internal weaknesses also matter? Here absolutism is relevant: the insistence that the Quran cannot be revised or challenged, and that Muhammad's example cannot be refuted but only followed.

Victimhood combined with absolutism increases the likelihood of conflict, just as militarism combined with imperial legacy does. These factors contradict the claim that Islam is a religion of peace.

*Transcript limit: her time expires here. She mentions two further factors she intends to discuss later, but does not present them in this opening. They are not supplied in this document.*

### Maajid Nawaz · For the motion

We are not making excuses for terrorism, suicide bombing or attacks on innocent civilians, including in Israel. Challenging extremism is my work. Muslims must do more to speak out and reclaim their faith from the minority who have hijacked it. This is also not a threat that rejecting the motion will provoke Muslims to attack you.

This is an appeal for peace. We want Islam, alongside other religions and people without religious faith, to contribute to peace. I ask you to vote in support of that effort.

For thirteen years I helped spread an ideology of hatred. I believed Islam mandated warfare and the creation of a state whose foreign policy would pursue conquest. I promoted that ideology internationally. Two experiences in prison changed my outlook.

First, Amnesty International adopted me as a prisoner of conscience. I had regarded its human-rights advocacy as a means of colonizing Muslim minds. Yet people I considered enemies defended me. Their refusal to let my hatred determine their response showed me goodness and changed my heart. Offering an olive branch can work.

I subsequently helped establish a counter-extremism think tank and began building a movement in Pakistan. Before its launch, it had gained 10,000 Facebook supporters. These Muslims refuse to let extremists monopolize Islam's definition or dictate that the world must be divided by war. I ask you to join that effort. <font color="#00b050">I also challenge the opposing panel's outlook, which I characterize as extending the description of war beyond Islam to religions generally.</font>

Second, in prison I encountered experienced jihadists who had abandoned their former ideology. I initially dismissed them as people who had sold out. I had real grievances from racist attacks and arrests, but I had also been arrogant and had failed to contextualize history, a failure I see in my opponent's presentation tonight.

Over four years, I learned Arabic, memorized half the Quran, studied theology and listened to these men, including people involved in Anwar Sadat's assassination. I concluded that Islam had been hijacked, abused and politicized by Islamism: a modern ideology owing more to post-First World War European fascism than to Islamic traditions. These former jihadists had reached the same conclusion.

Help us redefine this debate and reject the frameworks imposed by extremists. The civil-rights movement also challenged imposed frameworks. Vote to support those working for peace.

### Douglas Murray · Against the motion

Nawaz speaks about changing paradigms; I want to begin with facts. Today's reports describe the Times Square bomber, a network recruiting fighters in France, and a London transport worker seeking martyrdom for Allah. Such events must be considered when discussing Islam and peace.

If the motion concerned Christianity, people would raise the Crusades and contemporary Christian extremism. If it concerned Judaism, they would raise violent biblical passages and perhaps Israel's actions. Islam should face the same scrutiny. It is absurd to hold religions responsible when Christians or Jews act badly while treating Muslims' actions as having nothing to do with Islam.

Drawing on distinctions made by writers such as Bernard Lewis and Ibn Warraq, I distinguish three things: first, the Quran, hadith and Muhammad's life; second, the Sharia tradition; third, what Muslims actually do.

The first is bad. The texts contain substantial violence; violent verses outnumber and supersede peaceful ones. Muhammad is a bad moral example: he took child brides, abused a young girl, fought wars and beheaded Jews. I characterize him as a war criminal. None of this indicates peacefulness.

The Sharia tradition is also not peaceful. There is no school of Sharia to which this audience would want to submit.

There is hope in the third category, Muslims' actual behavior. Most Muslims do not do what these texts prescribe. They exercise their own moral judgment without relying on those books. But that does not rescue the first two categories.

I wish Khan and Nawaz spoke for Islam. The organizers invited clerics who would not debate Hirsi Ali, leaving people such as our opponents to defend the motion. Meanwhile, actual religious leaders teach very different lessons. Saudi Arabia's closed society and Iran's religious regime cannot be dismissed as isolated eccentricities. Influential figures such as al-Qaradawi broadcast antisemitism to large audiences. Khan is not the person speaking weekly on those networks; he is.

Islam is unstable. A thousand years ago the Mu'tazilites tried to reform it and were wiped out. It is many things, but calling it a religion of peace ignores difficult facts. That description elevates hope into truth. We should face the reality rather than mistake our wishes for an account of what Islam is.

## 3. Arguments in standard form

**P** = premise; **IC** = intermediate conclusion; **C** = conclusion. **[Implicit]** identifies a premise or conclusion supplied in reconstructing the reasoning. Unmarked statements are directly expressed or closely paraphrased from the opening.

These are reconstructions of the reasoning offered. Listing premises does not establish their truth, or show that they adequately support the conclusion. Where a speech asserts something without explaining it, that limit is retained. Several arguments below support one larger conclusion rather than independently proving the full motion.

### Zeba Khan · T010

#### O-K1. Islam and Western pluralistic life are compatible

- **P1:** My religious family and community encourage inquiry, interfaith respect and peaceful participation in American life.
- **P2:** My Islamic and Western identities have not been in conflict.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** These experiences count as instances of practicing Islam, rather than exceptions excluded from Islam by definition.
- **C:** The claim that Islamic and Western identities must conflict does not describe us.

This reconstructs her reply to the incompatibility view she attributes to opponents. It is not yet a claim about every Muslim's experience.

#### O-K2. Peaceful mainstream practice should determine Islam's characterization

- **P1:** Muslims and their interpretations are diverse; scripture contains both peaceful and violent passages.
- **P2:** Whether Islam is peaceful must therefore be determined by examining its practitioners and their interpretations.
- **P3:** According to the Gallup findings I cite, the overwhelming majority are peaceful mainstream Muslims; the violent group is a minority of a minority.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** The mainstream interpretation and practice should determine the religion's overall characterization.
- **C:** Islam should be regarded as a religion of peace.

#### O-K3. The violent minority is motivated by politics, not religion

- **P1:** Gallup finds that political outlook, rather than piety, distinguishes radicalized Muslims from mainstream Muslims.
- **P2:** Pape's research identifies political rather than religious motivations for terrorist actions.
- **P3:** The Tamil Tigers used suicide bombing for secessionist, not religious, objectives.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** These findings and examples justify attributing the Muslim violent minority's motivation to politics while excluding religion as the explanation.
- **C:** That minority is motivated by politics, not religion.

The exclusion of religion is part of her opening's wording. It has not been softened here into the more limited claim that politics also matters. Her opening names research but does not set out its methods or establish that all the populations described are equivalent.

#### O-K4. Peaceful Muslims speak for Islam despite limited visibility

- **P1:** Numerous Muslims express their faith through rescue, protection of others, assistance to women and rejection of terrorism.
- **P2:** Such Muslims constitute the mainstream; violent criminals twist the faith to justify their conduct.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** Mainstream practice expressing the faith should count as representative, whereas the claimed distortion should not.
- **C1:** These peaceful Muslims speak for Islam; terrorists do not.
- **P4:** Media coverage and conflict narratives obscure peaceful Muslim stories and voices.
- **C2:** Their limited public visibility does not establish that they are absent or silent.

The contrast between authentic expression and distortion is asserted in the speech; no independent rule for deciding authenticity is developed there.

#### O-K5. Vote to support moderate Muslims

- **P1:** Voting against the motion would reject moderate mainstream voices and signal agreement with terrorists' interpretation.
- **P2 [Implicit]:** The audience should support those moderate voices and withhold that endorsement from terrorists.
- **C:** Vote for the motion.

### Ayaan Hirsi Ali · T015 and T017

#### O-H1. Khan's example does not settle who represents Islam

- **P1:** Khan is an admirable example of a Muslim living a pluralistic, integrated life.
- **P2:** Shahzad also had an apparently integrated middle-class life but chose violence through a combination of piety and politics.
- **P3:** Who speaks for Islam remains unresolved.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** One person's admirable life is insufficient to establish that person's representativeness when rival claimants and interpretations exist.
- **C:** Khan's example does not establish that she represents or speaks for Islam.

#### O-H2. No monotheistic religion is a religion of peace

- **P1:** Islam's founding intellectual project rejected other gods and sought to unite humanity under one God.
- **P2:** Monotheistic religions have known lengthy periods of both peace and war.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** A religion with this combination of exclusive religious commitment and recurrent warfare should not be characterized as a religion of peace.
- **C:** No monotheistic religion is a religion of peace, including Islam.

The speech places the demand for one God next to the historical claim but does not fully explain the causal connection. P3 makes the proposed connection to the classification explicit; it is not a quotation from her.

#### O-H3. Militarism and imperial legacy contradict the motion

- **P1:** Jihad and successful military campaigns were central to Islam's conquests and success.
- **P2:** Conquest brought different ethnic and linguistic populations together, leaving a legacy that outlasts the empire.
- **P3:** That legacy creates a high likelihood of conflict, especially at communal fault lines.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** A religion whose history and continuing legacy have these conflict-producing features does not qualify as a religion of peace.
- **C:** Militarism and imperial legacy alone contradict the motion.

#### O-H4. Victimhood combined with absolutism increases conflict

- **P1:** Imperial defeat and humiliation can produce victimhood narratives that leaders exploit.
- **P2:** Some Muslims adopt the view that Islam is under siege and attribute its problems to external powers.
- **P3:** Religious absolutism prohibits revising the Quran or refuting Muhammad's example.
- **IC:** Victimhood and absolutism together increase the likelihood of conflict.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** This heightened conflict propensity is incompatible with characterizing Islam as a religion of peace.
- **C:** These two factors provide a further reason to reject the motion.

Her questions about scientific decline illustrate the need to consider internal flaws. They are questions in this opening, not a demonstrated causal history of scientific decline. She explicitly acknowledges actual colonial humiliation and says the siege mentality is held by a minority.

### Maajid Nawaz · T023

#### O-N1. Offering an olive branch can change an extremist's outlook

- **P1:** I formerly regarded Amnesty International and its human-rights advocacy as enemies of Muslims.
- **P2:** Amnesty supported me as a prisoner of conscience despite my hostility.
- **P3:** Its support challenged my view of my supposed enemies and helped change my heart.

- **P4 [Implicit]:** My experience provides a reason for others to support comparable efforts at outreach and peace.

This is grounded in his account of personal change. The speech also identifies study and encounters with former jihadists as contributors to that change.

#### O-N2. My former understanding of Islam as mandating war was mistaken

- **P1:** I formerly believed Islam mandated warfare and conquest, but I had failed to contextualize history.
- **P2:** In prison I encountered experienced jihadists who had abandoned their former ideology.
- **P3:** Through Arabic and theological study and discussion with them, I concluded that Islam had been politicized and hijacked by Islamism.
- **P4:** Islamism is a modern ideology owing more to European fascism after the First World War than to Islamic traditions; those former jihadists reached the same conclusion.
- **P5 [Implicit]:** This process of reconsideration and the resulting agreement provide reason to accept the distinction between Islam and Islamism.
- **C:** My previous belief that Islam itself mandated this program of warfare should be rejected; the program belongs to the ideology I now call Islamism.

The opening reports his intellectual journey and its result. It does not reproduce the theological arguments or historical evidence that established the result for him. The reconstruction therefore does not supply those missing arguments.

#### O-N3. Vote to help reclaim Islam and advance peace

- **P1:** An extremist minority has hijacked Islam's public definition and promotes a worldview organized around conflict.
- **P2:** Muslims have a responsibility to oppose this; my organizations and the developing movement in Pakistan are doing so.
- **P3:** People can change when others refuse to accept the hostile framework they impose, as my own experience illustrates.
- **P4:** We should help Islam join other religions and nonreligious people in building peace.
- **P5 [Implicit]:** Voting for this motion will assist that effort by supporting peaceful Muslims and challenging the extremists' monopoly.
- **C:** Vote for the motion as an act of support for peace.

The civil-rights comparison reinforces his invitation to challenge imposed frameworks. This practical appeal is prominent in the opening itself; it is not imported from his closing statement.

### Douglas Murray · T027

#### O-M1. Apply the same scrutiny to Islam as to other religions

- **P1:** In analogous debates about Christianity or Judaism, people would cite violent texts, history and contemporary actions.
- **P2:** There are contemporary cases of violence and militancy undertaken by Muslims, some explicitly invoking religious motives.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** Comparable religious claims should be assessed using comparable standards of relevant evidence.
- **C:** Such evidence must be considered in judging Islam; it should not be exempted from scrutiny applied to other religions.

The opening's newspaper examples illustrate P2. Their exact facts and their evidential connection to the religion remain claims to assess separately.

#### O-M2. The foundational texts and Muhammad's life are not peaceful

- **P1:** The Quran and related sources contain substantial violence; violent verses outnumber and supersede peaceful ones.
- **P2:** Muhammad's life includes warfare, beheadings and other conduct Murray condemns, including the abuse of a child.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** Foundational sources and an exemplary founder with these characteristics should not be classified as peaceful.
- **C:** The first category of Islam, its foundational texts and Muhammad's life, is not peaceful.

This retains the strength of his claims about predominance and supersession. The opening does not present the textual evidence for those claims.

#### O-M3. The Sharia tradition is not peaceful

- **P1:** There is no school of Sharia to which this audience would want to submit.
- **P2 [Implicit]:** The features that make those schools unacceptable to the audience also make the tradition non-peaceful.
- **C:** The Sharia tradition is not peaceful.

This part of the opening is brief and largely asserted. It does not give the particular legal examples that appear later in the debate, so none have been added here.

#### O-M4. Peaceful Muslims do not establish that the texts are peaceful

- **P1:** Most Muslims do not act as the objectionable texts prescribe.
- **P2:** They exercise their judgment as moral beings without having to refer to those texts.
- **P3 [Implicit]:** Peacefulness achieved through such independent judgment does not demonstrate peacefulness in the texts themselves.
- **C [Implicit]:** Hope in Muslims' behavior does not overturn the adverse assessment of the foundational sources.

His explanation of why Muslims behave peacefully is recorded as his claim. It is not presented as an agreed description of believers' own reasoning.

#### O-M5. The affirmative speakers do not outweigh contrary religious authorities

- **P1:** Khan and Nawaz offer an attractive peaceful account, but the clerics invited to the debate would not appear against Hirsi Ali.
- **P2:** Powerful states, leaders and broadcasters associated with Islam display coercion or promote hatred.
- **P3:** These are influential religious authorities and institutions, not isolated marginal figures.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** Their influence and authority are central evidence of the religion's actual public character.
- **C:** We cannot treat Khan and Nawaz's account as representative while dismissing these contrary cases as exceptions.

P1 records the contrast he draws. The speech does not establish that declining this particular debate, by itself, demonstrates a non-peaceful outlook.

#### O-M6. Calling Islam peaceful substitutes hope for the present evidence

- **P1:** The foundational sources and legal tradition are non-peaceful, on the assessments just presented.
- **P2:** Influential religious institutions and leaders reinforce the problem.
- **P3:** Peaceful Muslims provide hope, but past efforts at reform, such as the Mu'tazilites' effort as he describes it, have been defeated.
- **P4 [Implicit]:** These facts outweigh the grounds for describing the religion as peaceful now.
- **C1:** Calling Islam a religion of peace elevates a hope into a claim of truth.
- **P5 [Implicit]:** The audience should judge the motion by the evidence rather than by hope.
- **C2:** Reject the motion.

## Editorial limits

The streamlined speeches omit some detail, including full lists of Muslim denominations, personal acknowledgments, Murray's account of the bomber's timing error and some numerical or biographical illustrations. The bullet points and standard forms are based on the full opening passages, not on the shortened prose alone.

Names have been regularized for readability where the identity is clear. Murray's “muftazilites” is rendered as “Mu'tazilites.” The supplied transcript's garbled final wording in Nawaz's opening, “can we refute change,” is not assigned an exact corrected wording; his surrounding appeal to reject imposed frameworks is summarized instead. Hirsi Ali's reference to the scientific revolution is paraphrased without carrying over an apparent verbal transcription glitch.

No later argument, rebuttal or concession has been used to fill out an opening. In particular, this document adds no later discussion of Luther, particular Sharia adjudications, elections, apostasy threats or the American founders.
